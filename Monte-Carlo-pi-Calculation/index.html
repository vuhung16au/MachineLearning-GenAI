How it works:

1. A square with a circle inscribed within it is drawn

2. Random points are placed inside the square

3. The ratio of points inside the circle to total points ≈ π/4

4. Multiply this ratio by 4 to estimate π

5. The simulation stops when the error is less than the specified threshold

Mathematical Explanation of the Monte Carlo Method

The Monte Carlo method for estimating π relies on the relationship between areas and probability:

Geometric Basis:

Consider a square with side length 2r and a circle with radius r centered within it:

  • Area of square = (2r)² = 4r²
  • Area of circle = πr²
  • Ratio of areas = (πr²)/(4r²) = π/4

Probabilistic Interpretation:

When we randomly place points in the square:

  • The probability of a point falling inside the circle equals the ratio of areas: π/4
  • If we place N points randomly, approximately (π/4)·N points will fall inside the circle
  • If we count n points inside the circle, then n/N ≈ π/4
  • Therefore, π ≈ 4·(n/N)

Error Analysis:

The accuracy of this estimation improves as N increases. The error decreases proportionally to 1/√N, which means:

  • To reduce the error by a factor of 10, we need to increase the number of points by a factor of 100
  • The convergence rate follows the Law of Large Numbers from probability theory
  • With this simulation generating one point at a time, we can observe the gradual convergence towards π
